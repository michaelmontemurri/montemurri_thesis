\begin{abstract}
Accurate prediction of molecular optical properties is critical for the design of functional organic materials, yet many experimental workflows operate in data-limited regimes where current state-of-the-art graph neural networks (GNNs) are unstable or impractical. We investigate whether decoupling representation learning from downstream regression improves robustness in these settings. 

Across multiple optical property datasets, we systematically compare end-to-end Directed Message Passing Neural Networks (D-MPNNs) with hybrid strategies that freeze learned molecular embeddings and train gradient-boosted tree heads on top. In small- and medium-sized data regimes, hybrid models consistently reduce peak absorption prediction error relative to the original neural head, without requiring additional quantum-chemical descriptors or further end-to-end optimization. 

Using a nested experimental design, we decompose predictive variability of the hybrid approach and show that stochasticity in encoder training dominates overall instability across regimes, while the downstream head contributes a substantial fraction of variance in low-data settings. Transfer learning experiments further demonstrate that pretrained embeddings yield gains only when downstream datasets are chemically aligned with the pretraining corpus, with limited or negative benefit under significant distribution shift. 

Finally, analysis of the learned embedding geometry reveals alignment between dominant latent directions and established physicochemical determinants of optical response. Together, these results provide a mechanistic explanation for the effectiveness of hybrid modeling and establish gradient-boosted trees on frozen GNN embeddings as a statistically stable and computationally lightweight strategy for optical property prediction in data-limited chemical domains.
\end{abstract}
